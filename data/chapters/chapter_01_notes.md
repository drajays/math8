## RATIONAL NUMBERS
**Note ID:** `CH01-sec-rational-numbers`

##### RATIONAL NUMBERS

Any number that can be expressed in the form  $ \frac{p}{q} $, where  $ t $ and  $ q $ are integers and  $ q \neq 0 $ is called a rational number. For example  $ \frac{4}{7}, \frac{-8}{3}, \frac{5}{-11}, 7, -2, 0 $ etc. are all rational numbers.

## REMARKS
**Note ID:** `CH01-sec-remarks`

##### REMARKS

* Every integer $p$ (positive, negative or zero) can be written as $\frac{p}{1}$, so every integer is a rational number but every rational number is not an integer. For example, $\frac{-3}{5}$ is a rational number but it is not an integer.

* Every fraction is a rational number but every rational number is not a fraction. For example, $\frac{-3}{7}$ is a rational number but it is not a fraction.

The rational number  $ \frac{-p}{q} $ can be written as  $ -\frac{p}{q} $. Thus,  $ \frac{-2}{3} $ is the same as  $ -\frac{2}{3} $.

Two rational numbers are called equivalent if and only if they have same value.

The value of a rational number does not change if both the numerator and the denominator of a rational number are multiplied or divided by the same (non-zero) integer.

 $ ^{*} $ Every rational number can be written with a positive integer as its denominator.

Two rational numbers  $ \frac{p}{q} $ and  $ \frac{r}{s} $ are equal, written as  $ \frac{p}{q} = \frac{r}{s} $ if and only if  $ p \times s = q \times r $.

★ A rational number is positive if its numerator and denominator are both positive integers or both negative integers; and a rational number is negative if its numerator and denominator are such that one of them is a positive integer and the other is a negative integer.

* All terminating decimal numbers are rational numbers. For example,  $ 0.8 = \frac{4}{5} $,  $ 0.28 = \frac{7}{25} $,  $ 0.375 = \frac{3}{8} $ are all rational numbers.

## MULTIPLICATION OF RATIONAL NUMBERS
**Note ID:** `CH01-sec-multiplication-of-rational-numbers`

#### MULTIPLICATION OF RATIONAL NUMBERS

To multiply two rational numbers, proceed as under:

Multiply the numerators together and the denominators together, place the product of numerators over the product of the denominators.

Thus if $\frac{p}{q}$ and $\frac{r}{s}$ are two rational numbers, then

 $$ \frac{p}{q}\times\frac{r}{s}=\frac{p\times r}{q\times s} $$

---

## Result
**Note ID:** `CH01-sec-result`

## Result

$$\frac{3}{7} \times \frac{4}{5} \text{ means to find } \frac{3}{7} \text{ of } \frac{4}{5}$$

Since the rectangle AEFD represents  $ \frac{4}{5} $ of the square ABCD and the rectangle ABHG represents  $ \frac{3}{7} $ of the square ABCD, therefore the hatched portion (rectangle AEIG) represents  $ \frac{3}{7} $ of  $ \left(\frac{4}{5}\right) $ of the square ABCD) i.e. it represents  $ \left(\frac{3}{7} $ of  $ \frac{4}{5}\right) $ of the square ABCD.

We note that the square ABCD has been divided into 35 equal parts (rectangles) and the hatched portion consists of 12 such equal parts so that the hatched portion is $\frac{12}{35}$ of square ABCD.

Hence, $\frac{3}{7} \times \frac{4}{5} = \frac{3 \times 4}{7 \times 5} = \frac{12}{35}$

It follows that the product of two proper fractions is a fraction whose numerator is the product of the numerators of two fractions and whose denominator is the product of the denominators of the two given fractions.

The above result can be extended to:

If $\frac{a}{b}$ and $\frac{c}{d}$ are two rational numbers, then $\frac{a}{b} \times \frac{c}{d} = \frac{a \times c}{b \times d}$

## Commutative property
**Note ID:** `CH01-sec-commutative-property`

#### Commutative property

Observe the following:

(i)

 $$ \frac{-2}{3}\times\frac{5}{7}=\frac{(-2)\times5}{3\times7}=\frac{-10}{21}\text{and}\frac{5}{7}\times\frac{-2}{3}=\frac{5\times(-2)}{7\times3}=\frac{-10}{21} $$ 

 $$ \therefore\frac{-2}{3}\times\frac{5}{7}=\frac{5}{7}\times\frac{-2}{3} $$ 

(ii)

 $$ \frac{-4}{7}\times\frac{-8}{5}=\frac{(-4)\times(-8)}{7\times5}=\frac{32}{35}\text{and}\frac{-8}{5}\times\frac{-4}{7}=\frac{(-8)\times(-4)}{5\times7}=\frac{32}{35} $$ 

 $$ \therefore\frac{-4}{7}\times\frac{-8}{5}=\frac{-8}{5}\times\frac{-4}{7} $$ 

Thus, in general, we have:

If  $ \frac{p}{q} $ and  $ \frac{r}{s} $ are any two rational numbers, then  $ \frac{p}{q} \times \frac{r}{s} = \frac{r}{s} \times \frac{p}{q} $

This is called commutative property of multiplication of rational numbers.

## Associative property
**Note ID:** `CH01-sec-associative-property`

##### Associative property

Observe the following:

 $$ \frac{-2}{3}\times\left(\frac{3}{5}\times\frac{-5}{6}\right)=\frac{-2}{3}\times\left(\frac{3\times(-5)}{5\times6}\right)=\frac{-2}{3}\times\frac{-1}{2}=\frac{1}{3} $$ 

 $$ \left(\frac{-2}{3}\times\frac{3}{5}\right)\times\left(\frac{-5}{6}\right)=\left(\frac{(-2)\times3}{3\times5}\right)\times\left(\frac{-5}{6}\right)=\frac{-2}{5}\times\frac{-5}{6}=\frac{2}{6}=\frac{1}{3} $$ 

 $$ \therefore\frac{-2}{3}\times\left(\frac{3}{5}\times\frac{-5}{6}\right)=\left(\frac{-2}{3}\times\frac{3}{5}\right)\times\frac{-5}{6} $$ 

Thus, in general, we have:

If  $ \frac{p}{q}, \frac{r}{s} $ and  $ \frac{t}{u} $ are any three rational numbers, then  $ \left(\frac{p}{q} \times \frac{r}{s}\right) \times \frac{t}{u} = \frac{p}{q} \times \left(\frac{r}{s} \times \frac{t}{u}\right) $. This is called associative property of multiplication of rational numbers.

## Existence of multiplicative identity
**Note ID:** `CH01-sec-existence-of-multiplicative-identity`

##### Existence of multiplicative identity

Observe the following:

 $$ \frac{-2}{3}\times1=\frac{-2}{3}\times\frac{1}{1}=\frac{(-2)\times1}{3\times1}=\frac{-2}{3}\text{and}1\times\frac{-2}{3}=\frac{1}{1}\times\frac{-2}{3}=\frac{1\times(-2)}{1\times3}=\frac{-2}{3} $$ 

 $$ \therefore\frac{-2}{3}\times1=\frac{-2}{3}=1\times\frac{-2}{3} $$ 

Thus, in general, we have:

For every rational number  $ \frac{p}{q} $ ( $ q \neq 0 $), there exists a rational number 1 such that

 $ \frac{p}{q} \times 1 = \frac{p}{q} = 1 \times \frac{p}{q} $

The number 1 is called the multiplicative identity of rational numbers.
