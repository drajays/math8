## Cubes of rational numbers
**Note ID:** `CH04-sec-cubes-of-rational-numbers`

##### Cubes of rational numbers

If $\frac{p}{q}$ $(q \neq 0)$ is a rational number, then

 $$ \left(\frac{p}{q}\right)^{3}=\frac{p}{q}\times\frac{p}{q}\times\frac{p}{q}=\frac{p\times p\times p}{q\times q\times q}=\frac{p^{3}}{q^{3}} $$ 

 $$ \left(\frac{p}{q}\right)^{3}=\frac{p^{3}}{q^{3}} $$ 

For example:

 $$ \left(\frac{2}{3}\right)^{3}=\frac{2^{3}}{3^{3}}=\frac{2\times2\times2}{3\times3\times3}=\frac{8}{27} $$ 

 $$ \left(\frac{-3}{5}\right)^{3}=\frac{(-3)^{3}}{5^{3}}=\frac{(-3)\times(-3)\times(-3)}{5\times5\times5}=\frac{-27}{125} $$

## CUBE ROOTS
**Note ID:** `CH04-sec-cube-roots`

##### CUBE ROOTS

Answer the following question:

If the volume of a cube is 729 m³. What would be the length of its side?

We know that

volume of cube = (side)³

Let the length of side of the cube be x m,

then  $ 729 = x^3 $

To get the length of the side of the cube, we need to find a number whose cube is 729.

Hence, finding a number whose cube is known, is called finding the cube root.

Thus, we can say that:

The cube root of a number $n$ is that number which when multiplied by itself three times gives $n$ as the product.

Thus, if $m$ is the cube root of number $n$, then

 $$ m\times m\times m=n or m^{3}=n. $$ 

The cube root of a number $n$ is denoted by $\sqrt[3]{n}$.

 $$ \therefore\quad m=\sqrt[3]{n} $$ 

As we know that, finding the square root is the inverse operation of squaring. Similarly, finding the cube root is the inverse operation of finding the cube.

Thus, we have  $ 2^3 = 8 \Rightarrow \text{cube root of } 8 \text{ is } 2 (i.e. \sqrt[3]{8} = 2) $

 $$ 5^{3}=125\Rightarrow\mathrm{c u b e~r o o t~o f~}125\mathrm{~i s~}5\mathrm{~(i.e.~}\sqrt[3]{125}\mathrm{~}=5\mathrm{)} $$ 

 $$ 7^{3}=343\Rightarrow\mathrm{c u b e~r o o t~o f~343~i s~7~(i.e.~\sqrt[3]{343}~=7)} $$ 

Cube root of a number by prime factorisation method

the beginning of this chapter, we discussed that:

In a perfect cube can always be expressed as the product of triplets of equal prime factors.

Method to find the cube root of a number:

(i) Express the given number as the product of primes.

(ii) Make groups in triplets of the same prime.

(iii) Take one factor from each triplets of primes. Multiply them together; the product so obtained is the required cube root of the given number.
