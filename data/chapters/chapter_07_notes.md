## PERCENTAGE
**Note ID:** `CH07-sec-percentage`

##### PERCENTAGE

The word percent means ‘per hundred’ or out of hundred.

For example:

Utkarsh got 95 marks out of hundred marks. It means he got 95 percent marks.

Percent is denoted by symbol % (read as percent).

Thus, 95% means 95 out of hundred =  $ \frac{95}{100} $

Percentage is the numerator of a fraction with denominator 100.

---

## Converting a percentage into a fraction
**Note ID:** `CH07-sec-converting-a-percentage-into-a-fraction`

##### Converting a percentage into a fraction

To convert a percentage into a fraction, replace the % sign with  $ \frac{1}{100} $ and reduce the fraction to simple form.

For example:

(i)

 $$ 20\%=\frac{20}{100}=\frac{1}{5} $$ 

(ii)

 $$ 75\%=\frac{75}{100}=\frac{3}{4} $$

## Converting a fraction into a percentage
**Note ID:** `CH07-sec-converting-a-fraction-into-a-percentage`

##### Converting a fraction into a percentage

To convert a fraction into a percentage, multiply the fraction by 100 and put the % sign.

For example:

(i)

 $$ \frac{3}{25}=\left(\frac{3}{25}\times100\right)\%=12\% $$ 

(ii)

 $$ \frac{1}{3}=\left(\frac{1}{3}\times100\right)\%=33\frac{1}{3}\% $$

## PROFIT AND LOSS
**Note ID:** `CH07-sec-profit-and-loss`

##### PROFIT AND LOSS

Cost price. The price at which an article is purchased by a dealer is called its cost price (C.P.).

Selling price. The price at which an article is sold by a dealer is called its selling price (S.P.).

Profit. If the selling price of an article is more than its cost price, then the dealer makes a profit and price-cost price

Loss. If the selling price of an article is less than its cost price, then the dealer suffers a loss and loss = cost price - selling price

In solving problems on profit and loss, remember the following :

□ Profit = S.P. - C.P.

□ Loss = C.P. - S.P.

 $$ \Box\ Profit percentage=\left(\frac{profit}{cost price}\times100\%\right) $$ 

 $$ \Box  \cdot  Loss~percentage=\left(\frac{loss}{cost~price}\times100\right)\% $$ 

□ If there is a profit of $p\%$, then

 $$ \begin{aligned}S.P.&=C.P.+profit=C.P.+p\%of C.P.\\&=C.P.+\frac{p}{100}of C.P.=\left(1+\frac{p}{100}\right)of C.P.\end{aligned} $$ 

 $$ Thus,S.P.=\left(1+\frac{p}{100}\right)of C.P. $$ 

☐ If there is a loss of 1%, then

 $$ \begin{aligned}S.P.&=C.P.-loss=C.P.-l\%of C.P.\\&=C.P.-\frac{l}{100}of C.P.=\left(1-\frac{l}{100}\right)of C.P.\\&\quad\\&Thus,S.P.=\left(1-\frac{l}{100}\right)of C.P.\end{aligned} $$ 

If C.P. and S.P. are given for different number of articles, first find C.P. and S.P. of equal number of articles and then calculate profit or loss percentage.

## ☐ Overheads
**Note ID:** `CH07-sec-overheads`

##### ☐ Overheads

If an article is purchased for some amount and there are some additional expenses on transportation, labour, commission etc., these are to be included in the cost price. Such expenses are called overhead expenses or overheads.

For example:

If an article is purchased for ₹570 and ₹30 are spent on its transportation, then the total cost of the article = ₹570 + ₹30 = ₹600. If the dealer sells it for ₹645, then his profit = ₹645 - ₹600 = ₹45

 $$ \therefore\text{Profit percentage}=\left(\frac{45}{600}\times100\right)\%=\frac{15}{2}\%=7.5\% $$ 

Thus, overheads (if any) are always added to the original cost to get the total cost, and the

profit or loss percentage is calculated on the total cost price.

Example 1. John bought a watch for ₹540 and sold it for ₹585. Find his profit and profit percentage. Solution. C.P. of the watch = ₹540, S.P. of the watch = ₹585

 $$ \begin{array}{l} \text{Profit} = \text{S.P.}-\text{C.P.} = \text{₹585}-\text{₹540} = \text{₹45}\end{array} $$ 

 $$ \begin{aligned}Profit~percentage&=\left(\frac{profit}{C.P.}\times100\right)\%=\left(\frac{45}{540}\times100\right)\%\\&=\frac{100}{12}\%=\frac{25}{3}\%=8\frac{1}{3}\%\end{aligned} $$

## DISCOUNT
**Note ID:** `CH07-sec-discount`

##### DISCOUNT

Sometimes in order to dispose off old (or damaged) goods or to increase the sales or to attract customers to buy their products, shopkeepers offer goods at reduced prices. You must have seen banners like "Special offer, Huge sale, Discount up to 50%. Rush to avail of lifetime opportunity".

Discount means reduction in prices.

Marked price. The price printed on an article or written on a slip attached to it is called its marked price (abbreviated M.P.).

The marked price of an article is also called its listed (catalogued or advertised) price.

Discount. The amount deducted from the marked price of an article is called discount (or trade discount).

Usually discount is given as a percentage on the marked price. Thus, by a discount of 12.5%, we mean that the price of the article is to be reduced by 12.5% of the marked price.

Selling price. The price of an article after deducting discount from the marked price is called selling price or net price. It is the price of the article paid by a customer.

Thus,

 $$ \mathrm{selling~price}=\mathrm{marked~price}-\mathrm{discount} $$ 

In solving problems on discount, remember the following :

□ Discount is always given on the marked price.

☐ Selling price = marked price - discount

□ Discount = marked price - selling price

□ Discount percentage =  $ \left(\frac{\text{discount}}{\text{marked price}} \times 100\right)\% $

☐ If the discount is  $ d\% $, then

 $$  S.P.~=M.P.-\operatorname{discount}=M.P.-d\% $$ 

 $$ \mathrm{M.P.}-\frac{d}{100}\text{of M.P.}=\left(1-\frac{d}{100}\right)\text{of M.P.} $$ 

 $$ Thus,S.P.=\left(1-\frac{d}{100}\right)of M.P. $$ 

□ Successive discounts. Suppose a discount of 15% is given on the marked price of an article and further a discount of 5% is given on the reduced price, then we say that two successive discounts are given.

If two successive discounts are $d_{1}\%$ and $d_{2}\%$, then

 $$ S.P.~=~\Bigg(1-\frac{d_{1}}{100}\Bigg)\Bigg(1-\frac{d_{2}}{100}\Bigg)\ {~o f~M.P.~} $$ 

Example 1. A dinner set is marked at ₹2600. The shopkeeper offers 10% discount on it. Find the discount and the net price the customer pays.

Solution. Marked price = ₹2600, discount = 10%

∴ Amount of discount = 10% of ₹2600 = ₹$\left(\frac{10}{100}\times2600\right)$ = ₹260

Net sale price = M.P. - discount = ₹2600 - ₹260 = ₹2340

Hence, the customer pays ₹2340

## GOODS AND SERVICES TAX (GST)
**Note ID:** `CH07-sec-goods-and-services-tax-gst`

##### GOODS AND SERVICES TAX (GST)

The Central Government and the State Governments (including Union Territories) impose Goods and Services Tax (GST) to generate revenues.

In this section, we shall learn to calculate Goods and Services Tax (GST).

Goods and Services Tax (GST) is charged by the State (or Union Territory) Government on the sale of an item. It is collected by the shopkeeper from the customer and given to the Central Government and State Government.

It is calculated on the selling price of an item and is added to the bill.

---

**Note ID:** `CH07-sec-note`

##### NOTE

In case of any discount, first deduct the discount from the marked price and then calculate GST on the selling price.

Example 1. The cost of a pair of shoes at a shop was ₹ 670. The goods and services tax (GST) charged was 12%. Find the bill amount.

Solution. Cost of a pair of shoes = ₹ 670

 $$ GST=12\%\ of\ ₹670=₹\left(\frac{12}{100}\times670\right)=₹80.40 $$ 

 $$ \begin{array}{r l}{\mathrm{B i l l~a m o u n t}}&{=\mathrm{c o s t~o f~i t e m~+~G S T}}\\ {\mathrm{=}}&{{\mp}670+{\mp}80.40={\mp}750.40}\end{array} $$ 

Example 2. Marked price of a smart watch is ₹6000. The shopkeeper offers a discount of 5% on cash payment. If the GST is 18%, find the amount the customer has to pay in cash to purchase the smart watch.

 $$ \begin{aligned}solution.\quad&marked~price~of~smart~watch=\bar{\mathfrak{F}}6000;Rate~of~discount=5\%\\ \therefore\quad&Discount~=5\%~of~\bar{\mathfrak{F}}6000\\ &=\bar{\mathfrak{F}}\left(\frac{5}{100}\times6000\right)=\bar{\mathfrak{F}}300\end{aligned} $$ 

 $$ \begin{aligned}\therefore\quad&S.P.of smart~watch=M.P.-discount\\&=\text{₹}6000-\text{₹}300=\text{₹}5700\end{aligned} $$ 

 $$  Now~GST=18\%of\mp5700=\mp\left(\frac{18}{100}\times5700\right)=\mp1026 $$ 

 $$ \therefore\text{∴}Bill~amount=\text{₹}5700+\text{₹}1026=\text{₹}6726 $$ 

Hence, the customer has to pay ₹6726 in cash to purchase smart watch.
