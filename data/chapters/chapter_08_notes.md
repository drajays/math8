## SIMPLE INTEREST
**Note ID:** `CH08-sec-simple-interest`

##### SIMPLE INTEREST

Principal. The money borrowed (lent or invested) is called principal.

Interest. The additional money paid by the borrower to the moneylender in lieu of the money used is called interest.

Amount. The total money paid by the borrower to the moneylender is called amount. Thus,

 $$ amount=principal+interest. $$ 

Rate. It is the interest paid on ₹100 for specified period.

For example:

(i) Rate of $6\frac{1}{4}\%$ per annum means that the interest paid on $\mathbb{R}$100 for one year is $\mathbb{R}^{6}\frac{1}{4}$

(ii) Rate of 2.5% per quarterly means that the interest paid on ₹100 for 3 months is ₹2.5. However, if the time period for the interest rate is not given, then we will take the time period as one year.

Time. It is the time for which the money is borrowed (or invested).

Simple interest. It is the interest calculated on the original money (principal) at given rate of interest for any given time.

Simple interest is given by the formula:

 $$ Simple~interest=\frac{Principal\times Rate\times Time}{100} $$ 

In solving problems on simple interest, remember the following:

If P denotes the principal, R the rate of interest, T the time for which the money is borrowed (or invested), I (or S.I.) the simple interest and A the amount, then

 $$ ^{*}I=\frac{P\times R\times T}{100}\quad^{*}P=\frac{I\times100}{R\times T},R=\frac{I\times100}{P\times T},T=\frac{I\times100}{P\times R} $$ 

 $$ \mathrm{A}=\mathrm{P}+\mathrm{I}=\mathrm{P}+\frac{\mathrm{P}\times\mathrm{R}\times\mathrm{T}}{100}=\left(1+\frac{\mathrm{R}\times\mathrm{T}}{100}\right)\mathrm{P} $$ 

Example 1. A sum of ₹ 15000 is borrowed at a rate 15% per annum for 3 years. Find the simple interest on this sum and the amount to be paid at the end of 3 years.

Solution. Here, P (principal) = ₹ 15000, R (rate of interest) = 15% p.a.

 $$ \begin{aligned}T\ (time)=3\ years\\ S.I.\ (Simple\ interest)&=\frac{P\times R\times T}{100}=\text{₹}\frac{15000\times15\times3}{100}\\&=\text{₹}6750\end{aligned} $$ 

 $$ \begin{array}{l} \text{Amount } =\text{Principal } +\text{Interest } \\ \qquad = \text{₹ 15000 + ₹ 6750} = \text{₹ 21750} \end{array} $$ 

Hence, the simple interest is ₹ 6750 and the amount to be paid is ₹ 21750

# COMPOUND INTEREST

Normally the interest paid or charged is never simple. In simple interest, the principal remains constant for the whole loan period. However, in practice, the method according to which banks post offices, insurance companies and other financial institutions calculate interest is different from the one given above.

These institutions calculate the interest for one year. Then the yearly interest is added to the principal and the amount so obtained is treated as the principal for calculating the interest for the second year and so on. This process is repeated until the amount for the whole loan period is found.

The difference between the final amount and the original principal (money borrowed) is called the compound interest.

Compound interest in short is written as C.I.

**Note ID:** `CH08-sec-remark`

In case of simple interest, the principal remains constant for the whole loan period but in case of compound interest, the principal changes every year.

## DEDUCING A FORMULA FOR COMPOUND INTEREST
**Note ID:** `CH08-sec-deducing-a-formula-for-compound-interest`

#### DEDUCING A FORMULA FOR COMPOUND INTEREST

In the previous section of this chapter, you have learnt to calculate compound interest. The method used to calculate C.I. is very lengthy. Let us try to find a shorter way of finding compound interest. Suppose a sum of  $ \mathbb{P} $ is compounded annually at a rate of R% per annum for n years.

Principal for first year = P

Interest for first year =  $ \frac{P \times R \times 1}{100} = \frac{PR}{100} $

Amount at the end of first year = P +  $ \frac{PR}{100} $ = P $ \left(1 + \frac{R}{100}\right) $

Principal for second year = P $ \left(1+\frac{R}{100}\right) $

Interest for second year =  $ \frac{\mathrm{P}\left(1+\frac{\mathrm{R}}{100}\right)\times\mathrm{R}\times1}{100}=\frac{\mathrm{PR}}{100}\left(1+\frac{\mathrm{R}}{100}\right) $

 $$ \begin{array}{c} Amount at the end of second year~=~\mathrm{P}\left(1+\frac{\mathrm{R}}{100}\right)+\frac{\mathrm{PR}}{100}\left(1+\frac{\mathrm{R}}{100}\right)~\\=\mathrm{P}\left(1+\frac{\mathrm{R}}{100}\right)\left(1+\frac{\mathrm{R}}{100}\right)=\mathrm{P}\left(1+\frac{\mathrm{R}}{100}\right)^{2}\end{array} $$ 

Proceeding in the same way, the amount at the end of $n$ years

 $$ \mathrm{A}=\mathrm{P}\left(1+\frac{\mathrm{R}}{100}\right)^{n}\text{and C.I.}=\mathrm{A}-\mathrm{P} $$ 

Hence, the formula for finding amount at the end of $n$ years for principal $P$ and rate of interest $R\%$ per annum is

 $$ \mathrm{A}=\mathrm{P}\left(1+\frac{\mathrm{R}}{100}\right)^{n} $$ 

and Compound Interest (C.I.) = A - P

Example 1. Find the compound interest on ₹ 12500 for 3 years at 10% per annum compounded annually.

Solution. Given $P = \frac{1}{2} \times 12500$, $R = 10\%$ p.a., $n = 3$ years

 $$  A=₹12500\left(1+\frac{10}{100}\right)^{3} $$ 

 $$ \mathrm{A}=\mathrm{P}\left(1+\frac{\mathrm{R}}{100}\right)^{n} $$ 

 $$ =₹12500×\left(\frac{110}{100}\right)^{3}=₹12500×\left(\frac{11}{10}\right)^{3} $$ 

 $$ 12500\times\frac{11}{10}\times\frac{11}{10}\times\frac{11}{10}=16637.50 $$ 

 $$  C.I.=A-P=\varpi16637.50-\varpi12500=\varpi4137.50 $$ 

Hence, compound interest is ₹ 4137.50

## Rate compounded annually, half-yearly or quarterly
**Note ID:** `CH08-sec-rate-compounded-annually-half-yearly-or-quarterl`

#### Rate compounded annually, half-yearly or quarterly

In the previous part of this chapter we have used the term "Compounded annually". What does it mean? Why have we used it? To answer these questions, let us define the term conversion period.

Conversion period. The time period after which the interest is added each time to form a new principal is called conversion period.

or

The time from one specified interest period to the next period is called conversion period.

If this specified period is one year (i.e. the interest is compounded annually), then there is one conversion period in a year; if this period is six months (i.e. the interest is compounded semiannually or half-yearly) then there are two conversion periods in a year and half yearly rate will be half of annual rate; if this period is three months (i.e. the interest is compounded quarterly) then there are four conversion periods in a year and quarterly rate will be one fourth of the annual rate.

## SUMMARY
**Note ID:** `CH08-sec-summary`

## SUMMARY




Simple interest is the interest calculated on the original money (principal) at given rate of interest for any given time.

In simple interest, the principal remains constant for the whole loan period.

★ Compound interest is interest calculated on the previous year's amount (A = P + I).

★ In compound interest, the principal goes on changing every year.

★ Formula for finding amount at the end of $n$ years for principal $P$ and rate of interest $R\%$ per annum is

 $$  A=P\left(1+\frac{R}{100}\right)^{n}\quad and C.I.=A-P. $$ 

The time period after which the interest is added each time to form a new principal is called conversion period.

★ When interest is compounded semi-annually or half-yearly, then there are two conversion periods in a year and half-yearly rate will be half of annual rate.

★ When interest is compounded quarterly, then there are four conversion periods in a year and quarterly rate will be one fourth of the annual rate.

★ When the rates of interest for successive conversion periods are  $ R_{1}\% $,  $ R_{2}\% $,  $ R_{3}\% $....., then amount is given by

 $$ \mathrm{A}=\mathrm{P}\left(1+\frac{\mathrm{R}_{1}}{100}\right)\left(1+\frac{\mathrm{R}_{2}}{100}\right)\left(1+\frac{\mathrm{R}_{3}}{100}\right)\ldots\ldots\mathrm{a n d}\mathrm{C.I.}=\mathrm{A}-\mathrm{P} $$ 

★ When the total time is not a complete number of conversion periods then to calculate the amount we consider simple interest for the last partial period.
