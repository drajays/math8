In algebra, we use two types of symbols — constants and variables (literals).

Constant. A symbol which has a fixed value is called a constant

example, each of 7, -3,  $ \frac{5}{5}, -\frac{7}{2} $,  $ \sqrt{2} $,  $ 2 - \sqrt{3} $,  $ \pi $ etc. is a constant.

Variable. A symbol which can be given various numerical values is called a variable or literal. For example, the formula for circumference of a circle is  $ C = 2\pi r $, where C is the length of the circle and r is its radius. Here 2,  $ \pi $ are constants and C, r are variables.
