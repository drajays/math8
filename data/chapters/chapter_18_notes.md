## DRAWING 2-D REPRESENTATION OF 3-D OBJECTS
**Note ID:** `CH18-sec-drawing-2-d-representation-of-3-d-objects`

### DRAWING 2-D REPRESENTATION OF 3-D OBJECTS

Drawing surface is a paper, which is flat. When solids are drawn on a paper, the figures are somewhat distorted to make them appear three-dimensional. It is a visual illusion. Drawing 3-D shapes on 2-D i.e. on a plane surface needs some skills. The commonly used techniques of drawing solids i.e. 3-D on 2-D i.e. on a flat surface (paper) are given below.

---

## Steps
**Note ID:** `CH18-sec-steps`

##### Steps

1. Draw a rectangle of dimensions 4 units x 3 units to get the front face (see fig. (i)).

2. Draw the opposite face overlapping the first rectangle. This rectangle is 1 unit to the right and 1 unit above the previous rectangle (see fig. (ii))

3. Join the corresponding corners of the two rectangles by oblique lines to get the required cuboid (see fig. (iii)).




In the oblique sketch of a cuboid, we note that:

(i) the sizes of the front face and its opposite face are same, length 4 units and height 3 units.

(ii) the edges, which are equal to breadth 2 units, appear so in the sketch, though the actual measures are not so.

**Note ID:** `CH18-sec-isometric-sketches`

A sketch of 3-D shape in which the measurements of the sides are proportional to their actual lengths is called an isometric sketch. Isometric sketches are drawn on isometric dot sheets.

## Regular polyhedrons
**Note ID:** `CH18-sec-regular-polyhedrons`

##### Regular polyhedrons

A polyhedron is said to be regular polyhedron if all of its faces are regular polygons and the same number of faces meet at each vertex.

For example:




Cube

This polyhedron is regular. Its all faces are regular polygons and all the vertices are formed by the 3 faces.




Rectangular prism

This polyhedron is not regular. Its all faces are not regular polygons while all the vertices are formed by the 3 faces i.e. same number of faces.




Pvramid

This polyhedron with equal edges is not regular. Its all faces are regular polygons but all the vertices are not formed by the same number of faces. A is formed by 4 faces whereas B is formed by 3 faces.

Two most important members of polyhedron family around us are prisms and pyramids.

## Prism
**Note ID:** `CH18-sec-prism`

##### Prism

A polyhedron whose base and top are congruent polygons and whose lateral faces are parallelograms in shape is called a prism.

A prism is named after its base, if base is a triangle then it is called a triangular prism. Various types of prisms are given below.

---

## Triangular prism
**Note ID:** `CH18-sec-triangular-prism`

##### Triangular prism

A prism whose base and top are congruent triangles and lateral faces are rectangles is called a triangular prism.

---

## Rectangular prism
**Note ID:** `CH18-sec-rectangular-prism`

##### Rectangular prism

A prism whose base and top are congruent rectangles and lateral faces are rectangles is called a rectangular prism. It is also called a cuboid.
