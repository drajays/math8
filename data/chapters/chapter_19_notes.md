## VOLUME OF SOLIDS
**Note ID:** `CH19-sec-volume-of-solids`

##### VOLUME OF SOLIDS

Volume of a solid is the measurement of the space occupied by it.

To measure the volume of a solid, we have to compare it with some unit of volume. For convenience, the solid we choose for the unit of volume is a unit cube. If the side of the cube is 1 cm, then the unit is called cubic centimetre and if the side of the cube is 1 m, then the unit is called cubic metre etc.




Volume of a cuboid

Suppose you want to find the volume of a cuboid which has a length of 5 cm, a breadth of 3 cm and a height of 2 cm.

Start with bottom, which is a rectangle of size 5 cm by 3 cm. The area of this base is 15 cm² and so it is possible to cover it with 15 cubes, each with side 1 cm.

This forms the bottom layer of the solid and it has a height of 1 cm.

Now, on the top of each unit cube in the bottom layer, place another unit cube to form a second layer. The solid thus formed has a height of 2 cm and it contains $15 \times 2$ i.e. 30 unit cubes.

Hence, a cuboid with dimensions 5 cm by 3 cm by 2 cm has a volume of $30\ cm^{3}$.

## Volume and capacity
**Note ID:** `CH19-sec-volume-and-capacity`

##### Volume and capacity

There is a difference between volume and capacity.

Volume refers to the amount of space occupied by an object whereas capacity refers to the volume that a container holds.

Thus, if a container holds 100 cm³ of oil then the capacity of the container is 100 cm³. Capacity is also measured in litres.
